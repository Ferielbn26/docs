Cities might seem like the last place to look for ecology, but urban environments are teeming with ecological processes — and they're increasingly important to study. As more than half the world's population now lives in cities, understanding how nature functions, adapts, and sometimes struggles within human-built landscapes has become one of the fastest-growing areas in ecological research. EcoLearn's Urban Ecology lessons explore the science of cities as ecosystems, revealing how green spaces, wildlife, and human communities are deeply intertwined — and how smart design can make cities healthier for people and nature alike.

## What You'll Learn

The Urban Ecology modules introduce you to the ecological dynamics unique to cities and suburbs, including:

- **Urban heat islands** — how built surfaces absorb and retain heat, and how tree canopy and green roofs can mitigate temperature extremes
- **Green infrastructure** — the role of parks, rain gardens, bioswales, and green corridors in supporting biodiversity and managing stormwater
- **Urban wildlife corridors** — how fragmented green spaces can be linked to support animal movement and genetic connectivity
- **Invasive species in cities** — why urban environments are especially vulnerable to invasive plants and animals, and what that means for native species
- **Pollinator habitats** — how urban gardens, rooftop meadows, and roadside plantings can support bees, butterflies, and other essential pollinators

## Module Breakdown

The Urban Ecology category includes three modules, each focusing on a distinct dimension of how nature and cities interact.

| Module               | Key Topics                                    | Activities                    | Duration  |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|-----------|
| Urban Heat Islands   | Surface temperatures, tree canopy cover       | Heat map analysis             | ~30 min   |
| Green Infrastructure | Parks, green roofs, rain gardens              | Urban design challenge        | ~40 min   |
| Urban Wildlife       | Corridor mapping, human-wildlife co-existence | Wildlife camera review        | ~35 min   |

Each module stands on its own, so you can explore them in any order based on your interests or classroom needs. The Green Infrastructure module works especially well as a follow-up to Urban Heat Islands, since many green infrastructure solutions directly address the heat island effect.

## Urban Field Journal

The **Urban Field Journal** is an optional feature built into the Urban Ecology modules that transforms your everyday surroundings into a field study site. Using the Journal, you can log species sightings from your own neighborhood — a hawk perched on a lamppost, a patch of milkweed in a vacant lot, or a raccoon foraging at dusk — directly within EcoLearn.

Every entry you submit contributes to a shared **citizen science dataset** accessible to all EcoLearn users and educators. Your sightings are plotted on an aggregated map, which you and your class can analyze to identify patterns in urban biodiversity across different cities and neighborhoods. The Journal supports photo uploads, GPS location tagging (with your permission), and free-text observation notes alongside structured fields for species name, date, and habitat type.

You can access the Urban Field Journal from any Urban Ecology lesson page by clicking the **Journal** tab in the lesson sidebar, or directly from **My Tools → Urban Field Journal** in the top navigation.

<Tip>
  The Urban Field Journal doesn't have to stay in the classroom — some of the richest observations happen on your commute, in your backyard, or at a local park. The more you log, the more your data contributes to a real picture of urban biodiversity in your area. Challenge yourself to make at least one entry per week while you're working through the Urban Ecology modules.
</Tip>
