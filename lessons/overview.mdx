EcoLearn's lesson library is your gateway to understanding the natural world — from ancient forests and coral reefs to the green spaces thriving inside your city. Every lesson is built around real ecological science and designed to meet you where you are, whether you're a curious student exploring a topic for the first time or an educator looking for structured, standards-aligned content to bring into your classroom.

## How Lessons Are Organized

Lessons in EcoLearn follow a clear, four-level structure that keeps learning focused and progressive:

- **Modules** are themed collections of lessons grouped around a major ecological topic, such as Terrestrial Ecosystems or Aquatic Ecosystems. Each module tells a cohesive story about how a particular part of the natural world works.
- **Lessons** are the individual units inside a module. Each lesson introduces a specific concept, such as forest carbon storage or coral reef bleaching, and delivers it through a combination of content formats.
- **Activities** are hands-on exercises embedded within or following a lesson. They ask you to apply what you've just learned through interactive tasks, helping the knowledge stick.
- **Quizzes** appear at the end of each module to check your understanding and unlock your progress to the next module.

Working through a module from start to finish — lessons, activities, and quiz — gives you the strongest foundation before moving on.

## Lesson Formats

EcoLearn delivers content in several formats to match different learning styles and keep things engaging throughout a module.

**Video Lessons** are short, narrated videos (typically 3–8 minutes) that introduce or summarize key concepts. You can pause, rewind, and replay them as many times as you need.

**Interactive Readings** are text-based lessons enhanced with embedded diagrams, clickable glossary terms, and expandable callout boxes. They let you move at your own pace and dig deeper into terms or ideas that interest you.

**Drag-and-Drop Activities** ask you to arrange, sort, or label elements on screen — for example, placing organisms into the correct trophic level of a food web or matching animal adaptations to their biomes. These activities make abstract relationships concrete and tangible.

**Species Identification Challenges** present you with photos, calls, or descriptions of real species and ask you to identify them using visual cues or a dichotomous key. They sharpen your field observation skills and build a working knowledge of biodiversity.

## Lesson Progress

EcoLearn tracks your progress automatically as you move through modules. You don't need to manually mark anything as complete — finishing a video, submitting an activity, or passing a quiz all register instantly in your learner profile.

As you complete lessons and modules, you earn **XP (Experience Points)** that build your EcoLearn level, as well as **badges** tied to specific milestones — such as finishing your first module, identifying 10 species, or completing an entire ecosystem category. You can view your badges and XP total anytime from your profile dashboard.

Educators can also view class-wide progress from the Teacher Dashboard, making it easy to see which students have completed a module or where learners might need additional support.

<Tip>
  Use the **Bookmark** button (the ribbon icon on any lesson card) to save lessons you want to return to later. Your bookmarks are available under **My Library** in the top navigation bar, so you can pick up exactly where you left off.
</Tip>
