Water covers over 70% of Earth's surface and sustains some of the most diverse and ecologically vital ecosystems on the planet. EcoLearn's aquatic lessons bring these environments to life — from the sunlit shallows of a coral reef to the murky, tannin-stained waters of a mangrove forest. Whether you're studying marine biology, freshwater ecology, or the critical role of wetlands, these lessons combine rich multimedia content with hands-on virtual activities designed to make the underwater world feel immediate and real.

## What You'll Learn

The Aquatic Ecosystems modules introduce you to a wide range of concepts and field skills, including:

- **Marine zones and ocean structure** — how light, pressure, and temperature change with depth, and which organisms inhabit each zone
- **Coral reef ecology** — the biology of coral polyps, symbiotic relationships, bleaching events, and reef conservation
- **Freshwater food webs** — how energy flows through rivers, lakes, and ponds, and the role of keystone species
- **Wetland functions** — how marshes, swamps, and mangroves filter water, store carbon, buffer shorelines, and support biodiversity
- **Water pollution and human impact** — the sources and consequences of nutrient runoff, plastic pollution, and habitat degradation in aquatic systems

## Module Breakdown

Each module targets a distinct aquatic environment. Use the table below to plan your learning path.

| Module      | Key Topics                          | Activities                        | Duration  |
|-------------|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|-----------|
| Marine      | Ocean zones, coral reefs            | Virtual dive                      | ~50 min   |
| Freshwater  | Rivers, lakes, and ponds            | Water quality lab                 | ~35 min   |
| Wetlands    | Marsh ecology, mangroves            | Species mapping activity          | ~30 min   |

You can complete the modules in any order, but starting with Marine gives you a strong foundation in large-scale aquatic systems before moving into the more localized freshwater and wetland environments.

## Virtual Dive Feature

The Virtual Dive is one of EcoLearn's most immersive tools, and it lives at the heart of the Marine module. It places you inside a 360° underwater environment where you can explore a coral reef ecosystem in real time, encounter annotated species, and observe ecological relationships up close.

**To launch the Virtual Dive:**

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Marine Module">
    Go to **Lessons → Aquatic Ecosystems → Marine** and scroll to the Virtual Dive lesson card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Launch Virtual Dive">
    Click the **Launch Virtual Dive** button on the lesson card. The environment will load in your browser — no downloads or plugins required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate the Environment">
    Use your **mouse or trackpad to pan** the 360° view in any direction. Click the **arrow waypoints** to move between reef zones — shallow coral gardens, the reef wall, and the sandy seafloor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Interact with Species">
    Click on any highlighted organism to open its **species profile**, which includes its common name, ecological role, conservation status, and fun facts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Dive Log">
    After exploring, fill in the **Dive Log** to record the species you encountered and answer reflection questions. Submitting your log counts as your activity completion for this lesson.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The Virtual Dive includes narrated audio guides that you can toggle on or off, as well as a closed-caption option for all narration. You can replay the dive as many times as you like — each revisit may reveal species or interactions you missed the first time.

<Note>
  The Virtual Dive is best experienced on a **desktop or laptop computer** running a modern browser such as Chrome, Firefox, or Edge. While it is accessible on tablets and mobile devices, the 360° navigation and species annotation overlays perform best on larger screens. Make sure hardware acceleration is enabled in your browser settings for the smoothest experience.
</Note>
