EcoLearn's grading workflow is designed to keep your time investment as low as possible while still giving students meaningful feedback. Objective question types are scored the moment a student submits, so your gradebook starts filling in automatically. For the responses that genuinely need a human eye — written explanations, simulation analyses, open-ended reflections — a focused review interface lets you move quickly from student to student without losing context.

## Auto-Graded vs. Manual Grading

EcoLearn handles grading in two modes depending on the question type:

- **Auto-graded** — multiple choice, true/false, matching, and fill-in-the-blank questions are scored instantly when the student submits. The score posts to the gradebook in real time, and students can see it (if you've enabled grade visibility) without any action on your part.
- **Manual grading** — short-answer and open-ended questions, as well as simulation reflection tasks, require you to review and enter a score. These submissions appear in your **Needs Grading** queue, which is highlighted in your Educator Dashboard until you've cleared it.

## Reviewing Submissions

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the assignment">
    Go to **Assignments** in your class, then select the assignment you want to grade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Grade tab">
    Click the **Grade** tab at the top of the assignment page. You'll see a list of all students and their current submission status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open a student's submission">
    Click a student's name to open their full submission in the grading view. Auto-graded items already display scores; manual items show the student's response alongside an empty score field.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter scores and feedback for manual items">
    For each manual item, enter a **score** (0–100 or up to the point value you set) in the score field. Optionally, type a **feedback comment** in the text box beneath the response — students see this comment when grades are released.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and continue">
    Click **Save & Next** to record the scores and jump directly to the next student's submission. Repeat until you've worked through the full class.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Gradebook

The **Gradebook** gives you a full picture of class performance at a glance. Access it from your Class page by clicking **Gradebook** in the top navigation.

The gradebook is laid out as a grid — each **column** is an assignment and each **row** is a student. The **class average** for each assignment appears at the top of its column, and each student's **overall average** appears at the end of their row.

Use the filter controls to narrow your view:

- **By assignment** — select one or more assignments to focus the grid on those columns only.
- **By date range** — show only assignments with due dates within a specific period, useful for generating mid-term or end-of-term snapshots.
- **By student group** — if you've created groups within your class (for example, different reading levels or project teams), filter to see only that group's grades.

Click any individual cell to open the student's submission for that assignment directly.

## Exporting Grades

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Export dialog">
    From your **Gradebook**, click **Export** in the top-right corner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your export format">
    Select **CSV** for a plain spreadsheet, or choose a **LMS-compatible** format to sync directly with Google Classroom, Canvas, or Schoology.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a date range">
    Set a **start date** and **end date** to include only assignments due within that window. Leave the range open to export all grades for the class.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Export">
    Click **Export**. Your file downloads immediately (CSV) or posts to your connected LMS gradebook (LMS sync). For LMS sync, make sure you've connected your account under **Account Settings → LMS Integrations** first.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Every grade export includes the following fields: assignment name, student name, score, maximum points, percentage, submission date and time, and any feedback comments you entered during grading.
</Note>
